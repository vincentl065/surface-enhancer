How it works

The idea

The left view is your segment laid flat. The right views are two sections of the scan through the point under the mouse, 40 voxels above and below the surface. The orange line is the surface; the papyrus sheets are the bright bands. Where the line leaves its sheet, pull it back.

The original surface is never modified. Every gesture is saved at once and can be undone without limit.

In the left panel, every control has an ⓘ: hover it for what it does, how to use it and a small animation; click it to keep the card open.

Automatic buttons

Each one first computes a preview (nothing is written): numbers in the panel and a map on the page — orange hatching ╱ = moves towards the top of the section, blue hatching ╲ = towards the bottom, solid = 4 voxels or more (a change of sheet), white dots = proposed then refused. Apply writes it as one gesture (one Ctrl+Z). Follow the sheet and Remove steps start from the pinned point: click a spot where the surface is right first.

The score

0–100 per tile of 8 grid nodes: distance from the surface to the nearest sheet (35 %), sharpness of that sheet (20 %), steps between neighbouring nodes (25 %), stretch and shear of the grid (10 %), a neighbouring sheet too close (10 %). On the map: blank = good, dots = fair, hatching = weak, cross-hatching = bad, with the number and a letter for the biggest penalty (D distance, N sharpness, M step, G grid, V neighbour). The score helps you find places to look at; your eye on the sections decides.

Export, VC3D, ink

Export / download… gives a zip (tifxyz segment + offset field + journal) or writes a new segment into your VC3D volume package. A retouch is limited to ± 32 voxels around the original surface; to go further, export, then prepare the export as a new segment.

Re-read the ink: this tool does not ship an ink model. Render the layers of the retouched surface (python -m surface_enhancer render NAME), run your own model, then show its output here with ink-layer NAME "Label=prediction.tif".
